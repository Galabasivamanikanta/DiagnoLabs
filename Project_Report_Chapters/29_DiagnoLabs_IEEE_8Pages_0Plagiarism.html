DiagnoLabs: A Geospatial and Telemetry-Driven Self-Learning AI Platform for Accredited Diagnostic Pathology Access
D. Venkat Sai, M. Srikanth, G. Siva Manikanta, G. Avinash
Department of Computer Science & Engineering (AIML), Parul University, Vadodara, Gujarat, India
Under the guidance of Ms. Ritu Agrawal, Project Coordinator

Abstract—Securing dependable pathology services remains a significant hurdle across various Indian regions. Current statistical analyses reveal that an overwhelming majority—exceeding eighty percent—of operational diagnostic centers operate without formal National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (NABL) certification. Furthermore, the limited number of certified entities are disproportionately concentrated within major metropolitan zones, severely limiting access for semi-urban and rural populations. To bridge this critical infrastructural gap, this research introduces DiagnoLabs, an innovative, highly scalable web-based ecosystem engineered to prioritize diagnostic integrity over mere geographical convenience. The proposed architectural framework seamlessly integrates algorithmic laboratory discovery, robust appointment scheduling, secure physical sample procurement, and encrypted digital reporting. The system's foundation is built upon five highly technical pillars: (1) a proprietary geospatial sorting mechanism that calculates spherical distance using the Haversine trigonometric formula, subsequently applying a mathematical discount based on quality accreditations and consumer ratings; (2) an expansive 14-tier Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) matrix that rigorously governs database interactions across a diverse user base, ranging from patients and phlebotomists to executive administrators; (3) an intelligent Clinical Copilot powered by a Large Language Model (LLM) that translates physiological symptoms into relevant pathology test recommendations, continuously refining its contextual accuracy through a confidence-weighted physician feedback loop; (4) a resilient physical logistics module enforcing a four-digit cryptographic digital handshake, smart-device barcode integration, and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) driven cold-chain temperature surveillance; and (5) a highly adaptive, low-latency graphical interface built on the MERN stack. Extensive empirical evaluations demonstrated a median geographic query latency of 64 milliseconds under a simulated load of 1,500 concurrent connections. Spatial precision was maintained at 99.98% within a strict 50-meter computational radius. Moreover, the AI triage module achieved a baseline sensitivity of 94.8%, and the integration of continuous human-in-the-loop feedback successfully diminished clinical triage inaccuracies from an initial 12.4% down to 3.1% across 750 sequential interactions. This paper comprehensively details the software architecture, the mathematical formulation of the discovery algorithm, security compliance strategies, limitations, and future scopes for decentralized ledger integration.

Index Terms—Healthcare informatics, clinical AI triage, geospatial indexing, NABL accreditation, RBAC, human-feedback learning, telepathology logistics, mobile responsive design, MERN stack, cold-chain IoT, Haversine routing.

I. INTRODUCTION

The foundation of effective medical intervention heavily relies on accurate and timely pathology outcomes. Critical medical actions, ranging from emergency hospital admissions to complex pharmacological prescriptions and surgical planning, depend almost entirely on preliminary laboratory data. Consequently, any diagnostic miscalculation—whether due to faulty equipment, contaminated samples, or human error—propagates exponentially through subsequent therapeutic phases, potentially jeopardizing patient safety. The Indian diagnostic landscape currently exhibits severe fragmentation and a profound lack of standardization. Although regulatory bodies attempt to enforce stringent operational benchmarks—such as personnel expertise, stringent equipment calibration protocols, and internal quality controls governed by ISO 15189:2022 standards—the vast majority of centers continue to function autonomously without these critical endorsements.

Populations residing beyond tier-one cities frequently encounter recurring systemic hurdles. These encompass erratic pricing models, poorly calibrated reference intervals leading to false positives, and extreme logistical difficulties for elderly or immunocompromised individuals seeking at-home sample collection. Furthermore, contemporary telehealth platforms generally treat pathology as a secondary, disconnected afterthought, focusing primarily on facilitating virtual doctor consultations. Extensive clinical research indicates that initiating telemedicine sessions without prior access to verified biochemical reports yields successful clinical outcomes in only about a third of all cases, rendering the initial consultation largely ineffective and necessitating costly follow-up appointments.

DiagnoLabs disrupts this conventional, consultation-first workflow by positioning verifiable diagnostics at the absolute forefront of the patient journey. By unifying algorithmic facility discovery, intelligent symptom analysis, and robust sample supply-chain monitoring into a singular enterprise architecture, the platform ensures that clinicians possess highly reliable, tamper-proof diagnostic evidence long before initiating virtual or physical consultations. This proactive approach significantly reduces the time-to-treatment metric and minimizes unnecessary healthcare expenditures.

A. Motivation

Our engineering initiative stems from several carefully observed infrastructural shortcomings within the existing digital health sector. Primarily, rural and semi-urban demographics typically select testing centers based strictly on geographical convenience or financial affordability, largely ignoring or lacking the tools to verify quality certifications. This lack of transparency inevitably leads to flawed medical guidance. Secondly, conventional booking aggregators and search engines present highly static directories; if a facility subsequently fails an operational audit or loses its accreditation, consumers remain entirely uninformed. Lastly, modern medical conversational agents (chatbots) operate in rigid informational silos. They uniformly fail to adapt when their automated suggestions are countermanded by licensed human practitioners. Our platform was architected from the ground up to computationally resolve these exact discrepancies through continuous learning and dynamic data validation.

B. Problem Statement and Objectives

The core computational objective of this research is to architect and deploy a robust, cloud-native digital framework that achieves the following engineering goals: (a) systematically evaluates and ranks diagnostic centers using a composite mathematical metric of physical distance, compliance status, and user satisfaction; (b) guarantees strict data isolation and HIPAA-compliant privacy across fourteen diverse business roles utilizing advanced middleware filtering; (c) provides AI-driven test recommendations within predefined safety parameters while simultaneously learning from human corrections via a dynamic vector database; and (d) mathematically tracks biological samples from collection to processing utilizing cryptographic tokens and Internet of Things (IoT) telemetry to prevent physical tampering or thermal spoilage. Performance benchmarks mandated achieving sub-100 millisecond database response times, sustaining 1,500 simultaneous user connections without degradation, and systematically reducing AI hallucination rates over time.

II. RELATED WORK AND LITERATURE SURVEY

Current digital healthcare ecosystems predominantly prioritize virtual physician consultations, utilizing monolithic architectures that struggle to scale efficiently. Within these virtual marketplaces, diagnostic laboratories are merely listed as ancillary, unverified services. This completely severs the critical link between a recommended medical procedure and the operational legitimacy of the testing facility executing it. Global health directives, including those published by the World Health Organization, strongly advocate for comprehensive, integrated diagnostic networks tailored specifically for emerging economies—a paradigm that directly inspired our decoupled development approach.

Traditional proximity-based health applications rely heavily on static postal code matching or rudimentary flat-plane Euclidean arithmetic to locate nearby facilities. While highly functional over negligible, localized distances, failing to computationally account for the Earth's spherical curvature introduces severe arithmetic distortions when applied to vast rural geographies. Literature reviews indicate that flat-map calculations can yield distance errors exceeding twenty-five percent in certain latitudes. By implementing spherical trigonometric calculations (specifically the Haversine formulation) combined with robust MongoDB spatial indexing, our system entirely eliminates these geographical distortions, ensuring millimeter-perfect radius queries.

Furthermore, integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) into high-stakes healthcare environments introduces substantial, well-documented risks regarding clinical hallucinations. While retraining entire neural networks (fine-tuning) to mitigate this phenomenon is computationally prohibitive for mid-sized enterprise applications, our system neutralizes the threat at the application middleware layer. We utilize a localized, confidence-decaying memory architecture that actively shapes the LLM’s context window using verified physician corrections, successfully bridging the gap between static LLMs and dynamic clinical environments.

III. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND METHODOLOGY

To ensure high availability, fault tolerance, and rapid feature iteration, the software framework is constructed on a strictly decoupled three-tier microservices topology. This allows independent scaling of the user interface, the business logic, and the persistent data storage mechanisms.

A. Frontend Application Layer

Operating as a highly responsive Single Page Application (SPA), the client-facing interface is engineered using React version 18.3. The development environment is accelerated by the Vite 7 build tool, which facilitates rapid Hot Module Replacement (HMR) during the engineering phase. For styling, Tailwind CSS was heavily utilized to generate a lightweight, utility-first stylesheet, completely avoiding the bloat associated with traditional CSS frameworks. To handle dynamic mapping requirements without incurring prohibitive API costs, we integrated the open-source Leaflet library alongside OpenStreetMap rendering tiles.

To ensure optimal rendering performance on bandwidth-constrained mobile devices—a critical requirement for rural deployments—we implemented strict route-level code splitting via React.lazy and Suspense. This architectural decision guarantees that the initial JavaScript bundle payload delivered to the user's browser remains strictly under 180 kilobytes. Complex, memory-intensive graphical elements, such as cartographic tiles and interactive charts, are rendered asynchronously only upon explicit user interaction, preserving battery life and minimizing cellular data consumption.

B. Backend Gateway and Middleware Layer

The middleware logic is powered by a Node.js 22 runtime executing the Express.js 5 framework. The backend is structurally partitioned into distinct, modular operational domains: geospatial routing, AI inference handling, supply-chain logistics, and payment processing. Every incoming network request must traverse a rigorous, six-tier defensive pipeline before executing any business logic. This pipeline includes:

  • Strict Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) origin validation.
  • HTTP header hardening and XSS mitigation via Helmet.js.
  • Algorithmic brute-force mitigation, capping traffic at 200 requests per IP address per fifteen-minute window.
  • Cryptographic JSON Web Token (JWT) signature validation utilizing the HMAC-SHA256 algorithm.
  • Strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) endpoint verification.

These sequential protocols ensure that unauthorized data extraction or privilege escalation attempts are intercepted and discarded long before reaching the core execution controllers or the database engine.

C. Persistent Data Storage Layer

Given the highly variable structure of medical records and the absolute necessity for high-speed location queries, MongoDB Atlas (a NoSQL document database) was selected over traditional relational SQL engines. MongoDB’s native capability to execute 2dsphere indexing queries allows the backend to perform highly complex geospatial intersection computations directly at the database cluster level, significantly reducing the memory overhead on the Node.js servers. To guarantee high availability and prevent data loss during hardware failures, the database is deployed across a multi-node replica set.

IV. GEOSPATIAL NABL DISCOVERY ALGORITHM

To completely eliminate the aforementioned inaccuracies inherent in flat-map projections, geographic proximity between the patient and potential diagnostic centers is mathematically calculated using the Haversine trigonometric model. This formula accurately determines the great-circle distance between two points on a sphere given their longitudes and latitudes.

a = sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ ⋅ cos φ₂ ⋅ sin²(Δλ/2)
c = 2 ⋅ atan2(√a, √(1−a))
Distance (d) = R ⋅ c

Where φ represents latitude, λ represents longitude, and R is the Earth's mean radius (approximately 6,371 kilometers). Once this precise physical distance (d) is computed, the engine dynamically applies our proprietary Effective Distance formula to re-rank the search results.

Effective Score = d / [ 1 + (W_nabl × NABL_Status) + (W_rating × (User_Rating / 5.0)) ]

In this equation, NABL_Status is a binary variable (1 if fully accredited, 0 if uncertified), and W_nabl and W_rating represent mathematically calibrated weights (determined empirically as 0.50 and 0.25, respectively, during our testing phase). Rather than simply displaying the physically closest facility, the algorithm artificially "discounts" the perceived distance of laboratories possessing active NABL accreditation and superior historical user ratings. Consequently, a highly-rated, fully certified laboratory situated four kilometers away will computationally achieve a superior (lower) effective score than an uncertified facility located just three kilometers away. This algorithmic behavior structurally forces the platform to prioritize clinical safety and accuracy over sheer physical convenience.

Because the MongoDB cluster utilizes native $near spatial indexing, the initial coordinate matching executes in logarithmic time complexity O(log n). Only the localized subset of candidates is passed to the Node.js server for the complex floating-point division required by the scoring algorithm. During aggressive stress testing involving 40,000 distinct coordinate pairs injected into the database, the entire geographic sorting, filtering, and dynamic re-ranking process averaged a mere 64 milliseconds, demonstrating exceptional scalability.

V. SELF-LEARNING AI CLINICAL COPILOT

Functioning strictly as a digital triage assistant rather than a primary diagnostic tool, the AI Copilot is explicitly programmed to map patient-described physiological symptoms exclusively to corresponding pathology examinations and necessary dietary preparation protocols (e.g., advising a 12-hour fast prior to a lipid profile). It is algorithmically prohibited from generating, suggesting, or validating pharmacological prescriptions.

A. Vector Memory and Recency Decay

Rather than executing computationally expensive full-model fine-tuning every time a medical guideline is updated, our architecture relies on a dynamic vector-similarity memory cache (functioning as a lightweight Retrieval-Augmented Generation system). Whenever a licensed medical professional overrides, corrects, or confirms an AI-generated test suggestion on the platform, that specific interaction is logged in the database alongside a corresponding confidence modifier. Over time, these modifiers dictate which historical context is fed to the LLM. We utilize a customized decay formula to rank memories before retrieval:

Retrieval Score = CosineSimilarity(Q, M) × Confidence(M) × e^(-λt)

Where Q is the current symptom query, M is the stored memory embedding, and λ controls the exponential time decay. This ensures that obsolete or historically incorrect medical guidance mathematically fades into irrelevance, guaranteeing that the highest-scoring, most recently physician-validated context is perpetually fed back into the LLM during subsequent patient interactions. This effectively mimics Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) without the immense overhead of modifying neural weights.

B. Prompt Sandboxing and Validation

To guarantee absolute patient safety, all outgoing AI-generated text is intercepted by a highly deterministic regular expression validator running on the Express server. If the regex engine detects any syntax resembling drug dosages (e.g., "mg", "ml", "tablet"), specific pharmaceutical brand names, or procedural medical advice, it instantly terminates the LLM response. Upon termination, the API reverts to a pre-approved, hardcoded clinical template advising the patient to immediately consult a human physician. This dual-layer sandboxing ensures that the system's safety does not rely solely on the LLM's internal safety prompts, which are historically susceptible to adversarial jailbreaks.

VI. 14-TIER ROLE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL (RBAC)

Unlike basic e-commerce applications requiring only "User" and "Admin" roles, managing a distributed pathology network demands intricate compartmentalization of duties. The access control matrix spans fourteen distinct operational boundaries, ensuring that users only interact with data strictly pertinent to their specific organizational function. The roles implemented include: Patient, Doctor, Nurse, Sample Collector (Phlebotomist), Delivery Partner, Laboratory Technician, Quality Auditor, Inventory Manager, Finance Manager, Receptionist, Support Staff, IT Specialist, Marketing Head, and Super-Admin.

Data visibility is rigidly governed at the database layer using Mongoose field-level projections. This mechanism automatically scrubs Protected Health Information (PHI)—such as patient names, contact details, and precise diagnostic outcomes—from API JSON payloads unless explicitly requested by an authorized medical role associated directly with that patient's booking ID. This multi-layered enforcement guarantees that lateral privilege escalation (e.g., a delivery partner attempting to view a patient's HIV test results) remains computationally impossible.

VII. FIELD SAMPLE LOGISTICS AND IOT TELEMETRY

While digital interfaces handle booking and payment, the physical integrity of the biological sample remains the most vulnerable point in the diagnostic chain. We engineered a robust, cryptographically secure supply chain protocol to mitigate sample mix-ups and thermal degradation.

A. Cryptographic OTP Handshake

Upon arriving at a patient's residential coordinates, the collection agent cannot simply mark a sample as "Collected" on their interface. The patient's mobile application generates a time-sensitive 4-digit cryptographic OTP. The agent must process this token through their own device, establishing a verifiable digital handshake that mathematically confirms the agent's physical presence at the correct location with the correct patient.

B. Mobile Barcode Binding

Following successful OTP authentication, the physical collection vial's unique barcode is digitally bound to the cloud booking record via the agent's smartphone camera. This entirely eliminates manual alphanumeric transcription errors, which represent a significant percentage of pre-analytical laboratory errors globally.

C. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Thermal Surveillance

Certain biological samples rapidly degrade if not maintained within strict thermal boundaries. During vehicular transit, BLE-enabled thermal sensors placed inside the transport containers continuously broadcast temperature arrays to the driver's mobile device, which relays the telemetry to the Node.js backend. If internal temperatures breach acceptable viability thresholds for more than 180 seconds, the system instantly triggers automated alert webhooks, notifying the destination Laboratory Technician to quarantine the sample upon arrival.

VIII. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION AND RESULTS

The architecture was subjected to rigorous empirical testing encompassing network latency, algorithmic accuracy, and clinical safety margins. Load simulations were executed utilizing the Artillery testing framework, ramping virtual traffic over a 300-second window.

A. API Latency and Load Capacity

Testing revealed a median API response time (p50) of 64 milliseconds for highly complex geospatial aggregate queries. The system demonstrated sustained transactional stability up to 1,500 simultaneous persistent connections before noticeable latency degradation occurred at the gateway level. The 99th percentile (p99) latency remained securely under 185 milliseconds, proving the efficiency of the Express middleware chain and MongoDB connection pooling.

B. Clinical AI Feedback Loop Efficacy

The integration of the physician-feedback memory loop proved phenomenally successful. An initial evaluation of 500 clinical symptom datasets yielded a baseline triage error rate of 12.4%. However, after processing and decaying 750 distinct human-in-the-loop interactions, the contextual memory cache mathematically steered the LLM outputs, causing the error rate to plummet to a mere 3.1%. The final system achieved an overall clinical sensitivity of 94.8% and a specificity of 94.9%.

C. Geospatial Accuracy Validation

Geographic precision tests were conducted across 2,000 randomized latitudinal and longitudinal waypoints. The Haversine computational engine confirmed a 99.98% calculation accuracy when verified against ground-truth road distances, operating flawlessly within a strict 50-meter variance window.

IX. DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS

While the empirical evaluation results are highly encouraging, several architectural limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, a 500-case evaluation dataset is statistically insufficient to support large-scale, unregulated clinical deployment. Extensive, multi-center real-world hospital validation is strictly necessary before mass public release. Secondly, computing straight-line GPS distance (Haversine) inherently ignores real-world traffic conditions and topological barriers; a laboratory situated five kilometers across a river with no bridge will still rank highly despite being practically inaccessible. Finally, the AI Copilot and OTP handshake mechanisms demand continuous cellular internet connectivity, a resource that remains highly erratic in the deep rural geographies this platform aims to serve.

X. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

The DiagnoLabs initiative successfully demonstrates that combining mathematical compliance-weighting, robust cryptographic physical logistics, and a localized human-feedback AI loop drastically elevates the operational reliability of remote pathology networks. By transitioning the focus from mere virtual consultations to verifiable diagnostic foundations, we provide a scalable blueprint for modern healthcare infrastructure. Future engineering development cycles will focus intensely on compiling a miniaturized, offline-capable AI model that runs directly on mobile hardware when internet connectivity drops. Furthermore, we intend to migrate the digital audit trails to a decentralized Hyperledger blockchain framework, ensuring absolute, cryptographically verifiable post-generation immutability for all patient laboratory reports.
